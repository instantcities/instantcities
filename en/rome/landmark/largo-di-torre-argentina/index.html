Largo di Torre Argentina

Four temples of the Republic sunk below the street, beside the spot where Julius Caesar was murdered.

See it in 3D Watch the tour

Photo: Sotamies, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

When the area was cleared in the 1920s, four temples from the Roman Republic came to light, among the oldest in the city. Beside them stood the Curia of Pompey, where Julius Caesar was stabbed to death on the Ides of March, 44 BC.

The ruins are now also home to a colony of stray cats, looked after by volunteers in a shelter at the edge of the site. Visit it in 3D ›

More in Rome

ColosseumThe great amphitheatre of ancient Rome, opened in AD 80 for gladiator fights before some 50,000 people. PantheonA temple to all the gods rebuilt under Hadrian, with the largest unreinforced concrete dome in the world. Trevi FountainRome’s grandest fountain, where a coin thrown over your shoulder means you will come back. Piazza NavonaA long Baroque square on the outline of Domitian’s stadium, with Bernini’s Fountain of the Four Rivers. Roman ForumThe heart of ancient Rome: temples, basilicas and the Senate house, in a valley between the hills. VittorianoThe huge white marble monument to Victor Emmanuel II on Piazza Venezia, nicknamed the typewriter. Campo de’ FioriA lively square with a morning market and, in the middle, the hooded statue of Giordano Bruno. Column of Marcus AureliusA marble column carved with the emperor’s wars along the Danube, standing on Piazza Colonna since the 2nd century. Sistine ChapelThe Vatican’s papal chapel, where the cardinals elect the pope, under Michelangelo’s ceiling and his Last Judgment. VespaThe scooter that put post-war Italy on two wheels and rode through Roman Holiday. NasoniThe little cast-iron drinking fountains on Rome’s street corners, running with fresh water all day. SampietriniThe small black cobblestones of basalt that pave the streets of old Rome. CarbonaraPasta with egg, guanciale, pecorino and black pepper: Rome’s own dish, with never a drop of cream.
