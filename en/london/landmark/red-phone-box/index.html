Red phone box

The red cast-iron telephone kiosks of Giles Gilbert Scott, from the 1920s and 1930s.

Play London Watch the tour

Photo: Doyle of London, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

The red telephone box was designed by Giles Gilbert Scott, the K2 in 1924 and the smaller K6 in 1935 for King George V’s jubilee. Tens of thousands went up across the country.

Few are still used for phone calls; many have become tiny libraries, defibrillator boxes or coffee stands, and a few still stand on Whitehall. Ride through London in 3D ›

More in London

Big BenThe great bell in the clock tower of the Houses of Parliament, whose tower is now the Elizabeth Tower. Houses of ParliamentThe Palace of Westminster on the Thames, rebuilt in Gothic style after the fire of 1834. Westminster AbbeyThe church where English and British monarchs have been crowned since 1066. Trafalgar SquareNelson on his column, four bronze lions and the National Gallery along the north side. National GalleryBritain’s collection of European paintings, free to visit, along the north side of Trafalgar Square. Downing StreetNumber 10, the home of British prime ministers since 1735, behind black gates off Whitehall. Horse GuardsMounted troopers of the Household Cavalry stand sentry on Whitehall every day. London EyeThe giant observation wheel across the Thames, built for the year 2000. Westminster BridgeThe green bridge over the Thames beside Big Ben, painted the colour of the House of Commons’ benches. British MuseumThe world’s first national public museum, in Bloomsbury, with the Rosetta Stone and the Parthenon’s marbles. Black cabLondon’s taxis, tall enough for a man in a top hat, driven by cabbies who know every street by heart. Red double-deckerThe red two-storey buses that have carried London since the days of the horse bus. Changing of the GuardThe ceremony in which the King’s Guard hands over to the new guard, in red tunics and bearskins.
