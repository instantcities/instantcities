The Sacrifice of Iphigenia

Il sacrificio di Ifigenia

Unknown Roman painter, after a Greek painting, 1st century AD

King Agamemnon’s daughter carried to the altar, so that the wind would carry the Greek fleet to Troy.

See it in 3D The House of the Tragic Poet Pompeii

The Greek fleet could not sail for Troy: the goddess Artemis held back the wind until King Agamemnon sacrificed his daughter Iphigenia. Two men carry her to the altar, arms raised to the sky. The priest Calchas stands on the right with the knife; on the left Agamemnon turns away and hides his face in his cloak. Above, Artemis is already sending a deer to die in her place.

The painting was on the wall of the small garden at the back of the house, opposite the shrine. It is thought to copy a famous Greek painting by Timanthes, praised in antiquity for hiding the father’s grief rather than painting it. It was taken to Naples soon after the house was dug out in 1824. Stand before it in 3D ›
