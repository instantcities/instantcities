This is Delft

Delft lies between The Hague and Rotterdam in the province of South Holland, an old town of canals, bridges and gabled houses round one long market square. It is the town of Johannes Vermeer and of Delft blue pottery, the place where William of Orange lived and was murdered, and the resting place of the House of Orange. Today it is also a university town, home to the largest technical university of the Netherlands.

The city

The old town of Delft is an oval of canals, about a kilometre and a half from north to south. The Oude Delft, the canal the town was named after, runs straight through it, with the Oude Kerk on its bank, and to the east of it lies the Markt, with the Stadhuis at one end and the Nieuwe Kerk at the other. Round them a grid of smaller canals, the Voldersgracht, the Koornmarkt and the Wijnhaven, carries the boats past houses with step and neck gables.

Instant Delft takes you through the heart of it: over the Markt, along the Voldersgracht past the Vermeer Centrum, up to the Oude Kerk, down the Oude Delft past the Prinsenhof and back along the Koornmarkt. Like the real town, the game is flat.

Where it lies

Delft lies in the flat polder land of South Holland, between The Hague to the north-west and Rotterdam to the south-east, with the North Sea about ten kilometres away. The Schie canal links it to Rotterdam and the Maas, and the railway between The Hague and Rotterdam runs in a tunnel under the west of the old town.

The climate is mild and damp, shaped by the sea: cool summers, soft winters and rain in every season, with the wind blowing in from the west.

Places and landmarks

MarktThe long market square between the town hall and the Nieuwe Kerk, one of the largest in the Netherlands. Nieuwe KerkThe great Gothic church on the Markt, with a tower of 108 metres, where the princes of Orange lie buried. StadhuisDelft’s Renaissance town hall at the west end of the Markt, built round a medieval stone tower.
Oude KerkThe oldest church in Delft, on the Oude Delft, with a tower that leans about two metres over the canal. PrinsenhofThe old convent where William of Orange lived, and where he was shot in 1584. The bullet holes are still in the wall. Oude DelftThe oldest canal of the town, which gave Delft its name, lined with merchants’ houses and the Gemeenlandshuis. KoornmarktThe canal where the grain was traded, now lined with cafés, houseboats and terraces by the water. OostpoortThe last of Delft’s town gates, with two slim towers mirrored in the water. WaagThe weigh house by the Markt, where goods were weighed for the town’s taxes.

History

A town on a dug canal

Delft grew up in the 12th century along a canal dug through the peat, the delf, and in 1246 it got its town rights from William II, Count of Holland. Its brewers and cloth makers made it rich, and the Oude Kerk and the Nieuwe Kerk rose above its roofs.

A great fire in 1536 burnt most of the town, and much of what stands today was rebuilt after it.

William of Orange

In 1572 William of Orange, the leader of the revolt against Spain, made Delft his home and settled in the convent of Saint Agatha, the Prinsenhof. On 10 July 1584 Balthasar Gérard shot him there on the stairs.

He was buried in the Nieuwe Kerk, and ever since then the House of Orange has buried its dead there, in the royal vault below the choir.

The Golden Age

In the 17th century Delft was one of the six cities of the Dutch East India Company, and its potters began to copy Chinese porcelain in blue and white earthenware: Delft blue. Painters such as Johannes Vermeer, Pieter de Hooch and Carel Fabritius worked here, and the lawyer Hugo de Groot was born here in 1583.

On 12 October 1654 the town’s gunpowder store exploded, flattening the north-east of Delft and killing Fabritius among many others.

Engineers and students

In 1842 King William II founded a royal academy in Delft to train engineers, which grew into today’s TU Delft. The university spread over a campus south-east of the old town, and students now make up a large part of the town.

The old centre kept its canals and its gables, and in 2015 the railway, which used to cross the town on a viaduct, went underground in a tunnel.

Delft today

Delft is a lively town of about 105,000 people, a quarter of them students. TU Delft draws engineers from all over the world, and the town’s start-ups and research labs spread out round its campus.

On Thursdays the market fills the Markt, and on Saturdays there is a flower and antiques market along the canals. Visitors come for the Nieuwe Kerk, the Prinsenhof, the Vermeer Centrum and Royal Delft, and in summer the canals fill with small boats and the cafés put their terraces on the quays.
