The Alexander Mosaic

Mosaico di Alessandro

Unknown mosaicist, after a Greek painting, c. 100 BC

Alexander the Great charging the Persian king Darius at the battle of Issus, in a million and a half tiny stones.

See it in 3D The House of the Faun Pompeii

Alexander rides in from the left on his horse Bucephalus, bareheaded, and runs his spear through a Persian horseman. In the middle Darius, in his chariot, stretches out his hand towards him while his driver whips the horses round to flee. It is the battle of Issus of 333 BC, or Gaugamela two years later.

The mosaic is a copy of a lost Greek painting, made of about one and a half million stones, some no bigger than a few millimetres, in only four colours. It lay on the floor of an open room between the two gardens of the house. It was lifted in 1843 and taken to Naples; a copy lies in its place in the house. Stand before it in 3D ›
