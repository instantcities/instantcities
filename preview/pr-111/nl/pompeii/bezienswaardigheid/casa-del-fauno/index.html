House of the Faun

Casa del Fauno

The biggest house in Pompeii, a whole block, named after the bronze dancing faun in its atrium. The Alexander Mosaic was found here.

Foto: Gary Todd from Xinzheng, China, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

The house fills a whole block of about 3,000 square metres on the Via della Fortuna. HAVE, Latin for welcome, is set in the pavement at the door. It has two atria, side by side, and two gardens with colonnades all round them, one behind the other.

A small bronze faun dances in the rainwater pool of the main atrium; the one there now is a copy. Between the two gardens, in an open room, lay the Alexander Mosaic: Alexander the Great on horseback charging the Persian king Darius at the battle of Issus, made of about one and a half million tiny stones. The original faun and the mosaic are in the National Archaeological Museum in Naples. Bezoek het in 3D ›

Geschiedenis

The house was built in the 2nd century BC for a rich Samnite family, and was richer than most palaces of its time. It was excavated in 1830 and 1831. The Alexander Mosaic was lifted in 1843 and taken to Naples.

Als je het wilt zien

A copy of the mosaic lies in its place in the exedra between the gardens.

Meer in Pompeï

The ForumThe long square at the middle of Pompeii, closed to carts, with the Temple of Jupiter at its north end and Vesuvius behind it. Temple of JupiterThe main temple of the town, on a high podium at the north end of the Forum, with Vesuvius rising straight behind it. House of the VettiiThe house of two freed slaves who grew rich in trade, with the best-known wall paintings of Pompeii round its garden. The LupanareThe only building in Pompeii built as a brothel: a corridor of small cells with stone beds, erotic paintings and over a hundred graffiti. The Stabian BathsThe oldest public baths of Pompeii, with an exercise court, a swimming pool and rooms of cold, warm and hot water, for men and for women. The Great TheatreA theatre in the Greek style, set into a slope in the south of the town, for about 5,000 people, with the small roofed Odeon beside it. The AmphitheatreThe oldest stone amphitheatre that survives, at the east end of the town, built about 70 BC for some 20,000 people to watch gladiators. Thermopolium of Vetutius PlacidusA street bar on the Via dell'Abbondanza, with its counter of coloured marble and the jars for food and wine set into it. Garden of the FugitivesA garden by the south walls where thirteen people died under the ash. Plaster casts show where and how they fell. The BasilicaThe law court and exchange on the south-west corner of the Forum, from the 2nd century BC: the oldest basilica that survives. Temple of ApolloThe oldest temple in Pompeii, in a court of 48 columns beside the Forum, with bronze statues of Apollo and Diana as archers. Stepping stonesBlocks of lava across the streets, to cross without stepping in the water and rubbish, with the ruts of the carts between them. House of the Tragic PoetA small, richly painted house near the Forum, with the mosaic of a chained dog and CAVE CANEM, "beware of the dog", at its door. House of MenanderA large house with a garden of columns, named after the painting of the Greek poet Menander, where a treasure of silver was found. Fullery of StephanusA house on the Via dell'Abbondanza turned into a laundry, with the tubs where the cloth was trodden clean. Bakery of Popidius PriscusA bakery with four lava millstones, turned by donkeys, and the domed oven where the bread was baked. House of Octavius QuartioA house with a long garden of water channels, pergolas and fountains, almost the length of its block. House of Venus in the ShellA house whose garden wall is painted with Venus floating on a shell, with two cupids beside her. VesuviusThe volcano 10 kilometres north of Pompeii that buried the town in AD 79. It last erupted in 1944.
