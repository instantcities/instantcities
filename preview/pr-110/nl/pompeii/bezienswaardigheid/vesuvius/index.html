Vesuvius

Vesuvio

The volcano 10 kilometres north of Pompeii that buried the town in AD 79. It last erupted in 1944.

Speel Pompeï Bekijk de rondleiding

Foto: Jebulon, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Vesuvius rises 1,281 metres over the Bay of Naples, and you see it from every street in Pompeii. It is a cone inside the ring of an older, broken one, Monte Somma. In Roman times it was green and planted with vines, and nobody knew it was a volcano.

In AD 79 it threw a column of ash and pumice kilometres into the sky, which fell on Pompeii for hours, and then sent clouds of hot gas and ash down its slopes that killed everyone left in the towns around it. Pliny the Younger saw it from across the bay and described it in two letters; his uncle Pliny the Elder died trying to rescue people by ship. Rijd door Pompeï in 3D ›

Geschiedenis

Vesuvius has erupted dozens of times since. The eruption of 1631 killed about 4,000 people. The last, in March 1944, destroyed several villages and the planes of an American air base at its foot. About 600,000 people live in the zone that would have to be evacuated in another eruption.

Als je het wilt zien

A road climbs to a car park at about 1,000 metres, and from there a path leads up to the rim of the crater. The volcano and the land round it have been a national park since 1995.

Meer in Pompeï

The ForumThe long square at the middle of Pompeii, closed to carts, with the Temple of Jupiter at its north end and Vesuvius behind it. Temple of JupiterThe main temple of the town, on a high podium at the north end of the Forum, with Vesuvius rising straight behind it. House of the FaunThe biggest house in Pompeii, a whole block, named after the bronze dancing faun in its atrium. The Alexander Mosaic was found here. House of the VettiiThe house of two freed slaves who grew rich in trade, with the best-known wall paintings of Pompeii round its garden. The LupanareThe only building in Pompeii built as a brothel: a corridor of small cells with stone beds, erotic paintings and over a hundred graffiti. The Stabian BathsThe oldest public baths of Pompeii, with an exercise court, a swimming pool and rooms of cold, warm and hot water, for men and for women. The Great TheatreA theatre in the Greek style, set into a slope in the south of the town, for about 5,000 people, with the small roofed Odeon beside it. The AmphitheatreThe oldest stone amphitheatre that survives, at the east end of the town, built about 70 BC for some 20,000 people to watch gladiators. Thermopolium of Vetutius PlacidusA street bar on the Via dell'Abbondanza, with its counter of coloured marble and the jars for food and wine set into it. Garden of the FugitivesA garden by the south walls where thirteen people died under the ash. Plaster casts show where and how they fell. The BasilicaThe law court and exchange on the south-west corner of the Forum, from the 2nd century BC: the oldest basilica that survives. Temple of ApolloThe oldest temple in Pompeii, in a court of 48 columns beside the Forum, with bronze statues of Apollo and Diana as archers. Stepping stonesBlocks of lava across the streets, to cross without stepping in the water and rubbish, with the ruts of the carts between them. House of the Tragic PoetA small, richly painted house near the Forum, with the mosaic of a chained dog and CAVE CANEM, "beware of the dog", at its door. House of MenanderA large house with a garden of columns, named after the painting of the Greek poet Menander, where a treasure of silver was found. Fullery of StephanusA house on the Via dell'Abbondanza turned into a laundry, with the tubs where the cloth was trodden clean. Bakery of Popidius PriscusA bakery with four lava millstones, turned by donkeys, and the domed oven where the bread was baked. House of Octavius QuartioA house with a long garden of water channels, pergolas and fountains, almost the length of its block. House of Venus in the ShellA house whose garden wall is painted with Venus floating on a shell, with two cupids beside her.
