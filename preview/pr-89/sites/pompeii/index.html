The town, street by street

About 1,100 houses and shops along the streets of the excavation, with the Via dell'Abbondanza, the Via Stabiana and the Via del Foro, paved with basalt blocks.

The Forum and the amphitheatre

The Forum's two colonnades with the Temple of Jupiter at its end, and the amphitheatre, the oldest stone one there is, are modelled by hand. Vesuvius stands on the horizon where it really is.

On the map

  1. 1

    🏛️ The Forum

    The square at the middle of Pompeii, 140 metres long, closed to carts. Temples, the basilica, the market and the offices of the town stood round it, and people came to trade, vote and hear the news.

  2. 2

    ⛩️ Temple of Jupiter

    The main temple of the town, at the north end of the Forum, built for Jupiter, Juno and Minerva. Vesuvius stands right behind it. An earthquake in AD 62 had already damaged it, and it was still being repaired in AD 79.

  3. 3

    ⚖️ The Basilica

    The law court and exchange, on the south-west corner of the Forum, about 150 BC. It is the oldest basilica we know of, and one of the biggest buildings in town.

  4. 4

    🛁 The Stabian Baths

    The oldest public baths in Pompeii, with a gymnasium, a swimming pool and rooms of different heat. Most citizens went most days, and the stucco ceilings are still there.

  5. 5

    🏮 The Lupanare

    A small two-floor brothel with ten rooms, the best known of the town’s many. Its stone beds and the pictures and graffiti on its walls are still there.

  6. 6

    🗿 House of the Faun

    The biggest house in Pompeii, filling a whole block, with two atria and two gardens. A small bronze faun dances in its pool. The huge mosaic of Alexander the Great in battle was found here.

  7. 7

    🎭 The Great Theatre

    A Greek-style theatre cut into the hillside in the 2nd century BC, for about 5,000 people. The smaller Odeon beside it was roofed, for concerts and readings.

  8. 8

    🏟️ The Amphitheatre

    Built about 70 BC, the oldest stone amphitheatre there is, for some 20,000 people. In AD 59 a fight between the fans of Pompeii and Nuceria here ended in a riot, and Rome shut the arena for ten years.

About Pompeii

Buried
24 August AD 79, under ash and pumice from Vesuvius; dug out from the 1700s on
Size
About 66 hectares inside the walls, with some 11,000 people living there
Amphitheatre
Built about 70 BC for around 20,000 people, the oldest stone amphitheatre that survives
