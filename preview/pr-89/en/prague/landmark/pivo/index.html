Czech beer

Pivo

Czechs drink more beer per person than anyone; the pilsner was born in Bohemia.

Play Prague Watch the tour

Photo: Steven Walling, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

In 1842 the brewers of Plzeň, in Bohemia, made the first golden lager, the pilsner; today most beer in the world is brewed after it. The Czechs drink more beer per person than any other nation.

Prague’s old pubs pour it with a thick head of foam, and some brew their own, like U Fleků since 1499. Ride through Prague in 3D ›

More in Prague

Old Town SquareThe great square of the Old Town, with the Týn church, the astronomical clock and the Jan Hus memorial. Astronomical clockMade in 1410: every hour the twelve apostles pass its little windows while Death rings his bell. Týn ChurchThe Gothic church with two spiky 80-metre towers, behind the houses on the Old Town Square. Charles BridgeThe stone bridge of 1357 across the Vltava, lined with thirty statues of saints. Old Town Bridge TowerThe Gothic gate tower at the Old Town end of the Charles Bridge, one of the finest in Europe. KlementinumThe vast Jesuit college beside the bridge, with a Baroque library hall and an astronomical tower. JosefovPrague’s Jewish Quarter, with old synagogues and the Old Jewish Cemetery. Old-New SynagogueBuilt around 1270, the oldest synagogue in Europe still in use. RudolfinumThe neo-Renaissance concert hall on the Vltava, home of the Czech Philharmonic. Powder TowerA Gothic gate tower of 1475 at the start of the Royal Route, once used to store gunpowder. Prague CastleThe castle on the hill across the river, the largest ancient castle complex in the world; beyond this map. TramsThe red and cream trams that rattle along the river and across the bridges.
