Sourdough bread

The sour, chewy bread of San Francisco, baked since the Gold Rush from a living dough.

Play San Francisco Watch the tour

Photo: Edward Z. Yang, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Sourdough bread rises without bought yeast. The baker keeps a piece of each day’s dough, the mother, and feeds it flour and water; the wild yeasts and bacteria living in it make the bread rise and give it its sour taste. In San Francisco the bread is tangier than elsewhere, and one of its bacteria was named after the city, Lactobacillus sanfranciscensis.

The miners of the Gold Rush baked it in their camps, and old prospectors were called sourdoughs. The Boudin bakery has baked it since 1849, and says it still uses a mother kept alive since then.

More in San Francisco

Transamerica PyramidThe white pyramid of the Financial District, 260 metres tall, the sign of San Francisco’s skyline since 1972. Ferry BuildingThe ferry terminal at the foot of Market Street, with its clock tower after the Giralda of Seville, now a food hall. Coit TowerThe slim concrete tower on top of Telegraph Hill, built in 1933 with the money of Lillie Hitchcock Coit. Dragon GateThe gate into Chinatown on Grant Avenue at Bush Street, with green tiled roofs, dragons and two stone lions. ChinatownThe oldest Chinatown in North America, along Grant Avenue and Stockton Street, with lanterns over the street. Union SquareThe square of the big shops and hotels, named after the rallies for the Union before the Civil War. Cable carsThe last cable cars in the world still running in city streets, pulled up the hills by a cable under the road. Washington SquareThe green heart of North Beach, the Italian quarter, before the white church of Saints Peter and Paul. City LightsThe bookshop of the Beat poets on Columbus Avenue, which published Allen Ginsberg’s Howl in 1956. Golden Gate BridgeThe orange suspension bridge across the mouth of the bay, about five kilometres west of the game’s map. Lombard StreetThe block of Lombard Street on Russian Hill with eight hairpin bends between flower beds, west of the game’s map. AlcatrazThe rocky island in the bay, a federal prison from 1934 to 1963, built so that no one could swim away.
