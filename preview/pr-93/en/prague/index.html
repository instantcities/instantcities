This is Prague

Prague is the capital of the Czech Republic and the largest city of Bohemia, built on both banks of the Vltava. Its old centre came through the wars of the 20th century almost untouched, so Gothic towers, Baroque churches and Art Nouveau façades still line the same streets, under a castle that kings, emperors and presidents have ruled from for over a thousand years.

  • Charles Bridge and its statues, with Prague Castle on the hill behind it
  • Old Town Square, the Týn Church and the astronomical clock that has struck the hours since 1410
  • The Jewish Town, with its synagogues and old cemetery, and the city of Franz Kafka
  • Czech beer, pilsner poured with a thick head, and the red trams on the cobbles

About the city

Places, elements and landmarks

Old Town SquareThe great square of the Old Town, with the Týn church, the astronomical clock and the Jan Hus memorial. Astronomical clockMade in 1410: every hour the twelve apostles pass its little windows while Death rings his bell. Týn ChurchThe Gothic church with two spiky 80-metre towers, behind the houses on the Old Town Square. Charles BridgeThe stone bridge of 1357 across the Vltava, lined with thirty statues of saints. Old Town Bridge TowerThe Gothic gate tower at the Old Town end of the Charles Bridge, one of the finest in Europe. KlementinumThe vast Jesuit college beside the bridge, with a Baroque library hall and an astronomical tower. JosefovPrague’s Jewish Quarter, with old synagogues and the Old Jewish Cemetery. Old-New SynagogueBuilt around 1270, the oldest synagogue in Europe still in use. RudolfinumThe neo-Renaissance concert hall on the Vltava, home of the Czech Philharmonic.
