This is Barcelona

Barcelona is the capital of Catalonia and Spain’s second city, a Mediterranean port between the mountains and the sea. Its medieval Gothic Quarter, the buildings of Antoni Gaudí and the other Modernista architects, and La Rambla running down to the harbour have made it one of the most visited cities in Europe.

  • Gaudí’s Sagrada Família, Park Güell, Casa Batlló and La Pedrera
  • La Rambla, the Boqueria market and the lanes of the Gothic Quarter
  • FC Barcelona and the 1992 Olympic Games
  • Tapas, pa amb tomàquet and the human towers of the castellers

About the city

Places, elements and landmarks

La RamblaThe tree-lined promenade from Plaça de Catalunya down to the sea, Barcelona’s busiest street. Plaça de CatalunyaThe great square where the old town meets the 19th-century Eixample. La BoqueriaBarcelona’s most famous market, under an iron roof just off the Rambla. Gran Teatre del LiceuThe opera house on the Rambla, opened in 1847 and rebuilt after a fire in 1994. Plaça ReialAn arcaded square with palm trees and lamps designed by a young Antoni Gaudí. CathedralThe Gothic cathedral of Santa Eulàlia, with thirteen white geese in its cloister. Plaça de Sant JaumeThe square where the government of Catalonia and the city hall face each other, on the old Roman forum. Columbus MonumentColumbus on a 60-metre column at the bottom of the Rambla, pointing out to sea. Gothic QuarterThe maze of narrow medieval lanes at the heart of the old town, on top of Roman Barcino.
