Notre-Dame

The Gothic cathedral on the Île de la Cité, begun in 1163 and open again since the fire of 2019.

See it in 3D Watch the tour

Photo: Ali Sabbagh, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Notre-Dame de Paris was begun in 1163 and finished almost two centuries later, with its twin towers, its great rose windows and the flying buttresses that hold up its walls. Victor Hugo’s novel of 1831 made it famous all over the world and helped save it from decay.

On 15 April 2019 fire destroyed its roof and its spire. After five years of work it opened again in December 2024, with a new spire built to the old design. Visit it in 3D ›

More in Paris

Eiffel TowerGustave Eiffel’s iron tower on the Champ de Mars, built for the World’s Fair of 1889 and the symbol of Paris ever since. Sainte-ChapelleLouis IX’s royal chapel of 1248, its walls almost all stained glass. LouvreA royal palace turned into the world’s most visited museum, with the glass pyramid in its courtyard. Pont NeufThe “new bridge”, which is in fact the oldest bridge still standing across the Seine in Paris. Hôtel de VilleThe city hall of Paris, on this spot since 1357 and rebuilt after the Commune burned it in 1871. ConciergerieA medieval royal palace on the island, later the prison where Marie-Antoinette awaited the guillotine. Tour Saint-JacquesA lone Gothic bell tower, all that remains of a church on the pilgrims’ road to Santiago. Centre PompidouThe museum of modern art with its pipes, ducts and escalators on the outside, opened in 1977. BouquinistesThe green boxes of second-hand booksellers along the quays of the Seine. Métro entrancesThe green Art Nouveau entrances of the Paris Métro, drawn by Hector Guimard around 1900. Bateaux-mouchesThe sightseeing boats that glide up and down the Seine, past Notre-Dame and under the bridges. CroissantThe flaky, buttery crescent of a Paris breakfast, from the boulangerie on every corner.
