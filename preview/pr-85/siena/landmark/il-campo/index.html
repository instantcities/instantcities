Piazza del Campo

Il Campo

The shell-shaped square at the heart of Siena, paved in red brick and sloping down to the town hall, where the Palio is run.

See it in 3D Watch the tour

Photo: Andrzej Otrębski, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

The Campo lies where Siena’s three hills meet, on the old marketplace below them. The city government of the Nine had it paved in red brick from 1327 to 1349, in nine fan-shaped slices of travertine, one for each of its members, so the square opens like a shell and slopes down to the Palazzo Pubblico.

The palaces round it had to follow the same rules for their windows and their height, so the square still looks as if one hand drew it. Twice every summer it fills up with earth and people for the Palio, and on any other day the Sienese sit on its bricks in the sun. Visit it in 3D ›

More in Siena

Palazzo PubblicoSiena’s Gothic town hall of brick and stone at the foot of the Campo, with the most famous frescoes of good and bad government. Torre del MangiaThe slender brick tower of the town hall, 88 metres tall, with the stone crown of its bell chamber over the Campo. Fonte GaiaThe marble fountain at the top of the Campo, where the water of Siena’s underground aqueducts first reached the square. Duomo di SienaThe cathedral on the highest hill of the town, striped in white and dark green marble, with a floor of inlaid marble pictures. FacciatoneThe bare front of the vast new cathedral that Siena began in 1339 and gave up after the Black Death. Santa Maria della ScalaOne of the oldest hospitals of Europe, opposite the cathedral, which looked after pilgrims and foundlings for nearly a thousand years. Loggia della MercanziaThe 15th-century loggia of the merchants’ court, at the crossing where Siena’s three main streets meet above the Campo. Piazza SalimbeniThe small square of the palaces of the Monte dei Paschi, the oldest bank in the world still in business. San DomenicoThe great brick church of the Dominicans on its own hill, where Saint Catherine of Siena prayed, and where her head is kept. Fortezza MediceaThe fortress that Florence built after it took Siena in 1555, now a park on its walls above the town. Palio di SienaThe wild horse race round the Campo, run twice every summer between the contrade, the neighbourhoods of the town. PanforteSiena’s dense cake of honey, almonds, candied fruit and spices, baked here since the Middle Ages.
