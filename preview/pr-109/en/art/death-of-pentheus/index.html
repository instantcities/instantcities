The Death of Pentheus

La morte di Penteo

Unknown Roman painter, c. AD 62–79

King Pentheus of Thebes torn apart by the women who followed Bacchus, among them his own mother.

See it in 3D The House of the Vettii Pompeii

Pentheus, king of Thebes, forbade the worship of the new god Bacchus and went up the mountain to spy on the women who danced for him. They found him. In the painting he has fallen to his knees in the middle, and the women close in on him from both sides; the one pulling his hair is his mother Agave, who in her frenzy took him for a lion.

It is one of three big pictures on the walls of a small room off the garden, all of them of cruel myths: next to it the infant Hercules strangles the snakes sent to kill him, and Dirce is tied to a bull. Stand before it in 3D ›
