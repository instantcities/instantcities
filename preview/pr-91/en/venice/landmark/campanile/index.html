Campanile di San Marco

The bell tower of St Mark’s, which fell down in 1902 and was rebuilt “where it was, as it was”.

See it in 3D Watch the tour

Photo: This Photo was taken by Wolfgang Moroder. Feel free to us..., CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

The brick bell tower rises almost 99 metres over the square. On 14 July 1902 it collapsed into a heap of rubble; by a miracle only the caretaker’s cat died.

Venice rebuilt it exactly as before, “com’era, dov’era”, and it opened again in 1912. A lift takes you to the top, where Galileo once showed his telescope to the doge. Visit it in 3D ›

More in Venice

Piazza San MarcoThe only square in Venice called a piazza, ringed by arcades and opening onto the lagoon. St Mark’s BasilicaBuilt for the relics of Saint Mark, covered inside with golden mosaics. Doge’s PalaceThe pink and white Gothic palace from which the doges ruled the Republic of Venice. Bridge of SighsThe covered stone bridge between the Doge’s Palace and the prisons, built in 1600. Rialto BridgeThe stone bridge of 1591 over the Grand Canal, with shops along its arch. Grand CanalThe great S-shaped waterway through the middle of Venice, lined with palaces. MercerieThe chain of narrow shopping lanes from St Mark’s to the Rialto, the race route in the game. La FeniceVenice’s opera house, which has risen from its ashes more than once, as its name promises. GondolaThe long black boat of Venice, rowed standing up with a single oar. VaporettoThe water bus of Venice, chugging up and down the Grand Canal from stop to stop. CarnivalTwo weeks of masks and costumes before Lent, filling the squares of Venice. Acqua altaThe high tides that flood St Mark’s Square, now held back by the MOSE barriers.
