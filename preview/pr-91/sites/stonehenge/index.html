On the map

  1. 1

    🪨 The sarsen circle

    Thirty great sandstone blocks from the Marlborough Downs, 25 kilometres away, set up about 2500 BC. Each is about 4 metres high and weighs some 25 tonnes. A ring of lintels once ran across their tops, and they were smoothed and fitted with joints like carpentry.

  2. 2

    ⛰️ The trilithons

    Five pairs of the biggest stones, each with a lintel over it, stand in a horseshoe open to the north-east. They are 6 to 7.3 metres high. Three still stand whole; the great one in the middle fell long ago.

  3. 3

    🔵 The bluestones

    The smaller stones, about 2 metres high, came from the Preseli Hills in Wales, some 250 kilometres away. Nobody knows for sure how they were moved. They were set up and taken down and moved around several times.

  4. 4

    🌅 The Heel Stone

    A rough, leaning stone 77 metres out along the axis. On midsummer morning the sun rises over its tip and sends its light down the Avenue into the middle of the circle. At midwinter the sun sets on the opposite side.

  5. 5

    🪦 The Slaughter Stone

    A big fallen stone by the entrance, with pits in its top that filled with rainwater and turned red with the iron in the rock. The name was made up in the 1600s and has nothing to do with sacrifices.

  6. 6

    ⚪ The Aubrey holes

    A ring of 56 pits, each about a metre wide, round the stones, under the bank. Some held wooden posts, and later cremated remains. They were found in the 1600s by John Aubrey and are marked on the turf with white discs.

  7. 7

    🛤️ The Avenue

    A broad path between two low banks that runs from the entrance for nearly three kilometres, to the River Avon. People walked it towards the circle. It was made about 2400 BC.

About Stonehenge

Built
From about 3000 BC, in stages over some 1,500 years
Sarsens
30 uprights and 30 lintels in a circle 33 metres across; 17 uprights and 6 lintels still stand
Bluestones
About 2 metres high, from the Preseli Hills in Wales, some 250 kilometres away
