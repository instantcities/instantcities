This is Vienna

Vienna is the capital of Austria and, for more than six hundred years, was the seat of the Habsburgs, who ruled a great part of Europe from its palaces. Mozart, Beethoven and Strauss made music here, Freud and Klimt changed how people saw the mind and art, and the city still keeps its imperial palaces, its Ringstraße and its old coffee houses.

  • St Stephen’s Cathedral and its tall Gothic spire in the middle of the old town
  • The Hofburg and Schönbrunn, the winter and summer palaces of the Habsburg emperors
  • Classical music, from the State Opera and the waltz to the Philharmonic’s New Year’s Concert
  • Coffee houses, Sachertorte and Wiener Schnitzel

About the city

Places, elements and landmarks

St Stephen’s CathedralVienna’s Gothic cathedral, with its 136-metre south tower and a roof of glazed tiles in zigzags. GrabenVienna’s grand shopping street on the line of the Roman ditch, with the Plague Column in the middle. Plague ColumnThe Baroque column of clouds and angels put up after the plague of 1679. HofburgThe Habsburgs’ winter palace for six centuries, now the seat of Austria’s president. MichaelerplatzThe round square at the Hofburg’s gate, where Roman ruins and Adolf Loos’ plain house face the palace. Spanish Riding SchoolThe white Lipizzaner stallions dance in a Baroque riding hall inside the Hofburg. State OperaOpened in 1869, the first great building on the Ringstraße and one of the world’s great opera houses. AlbertinaA Habsburg palace with one of the world’s largest collections of prints and drawings, Dürer’s Hare among them. BurgtheaterOne of the most important German-language theatres, on the Ringstraße, opposite the town hall.
