Gioco del Ponte

On the last Saturday of June the north and the south of Pisa push a cart across the Ponte di Mezzo, after a parade in Renaissance costume.

Play Pisa Watch the tour

The Game of the Bridge goes back to the battles of the Middle Ages, when the two halves of the city fought on the Ponte di Mezzo with wooden shields. Today the north bank, Tramontana, and the south bank, Mezzogiorno, each field six quarters, and their teams push a heavy cart along a rail laid across the bridge.

The side that drives the cart into the other’s half wins the round, and the side that wins most of the rounds takes the day. Before the game hundreds of people parade along the Lungarni in Renaissance costume and armour. Ride through Pisa in 3D ›

More in Pisa

Leaning Tower of PisaThe cathedral’s bell tower, which started to lean before its third floor was finished and has leaned ever since, some four degrees today. Duomo di PisaThe marble cathedral of 1064, paid for with the spoils of a raid on Palermo, the first great building of the Pisan Romanesque. BaptisteryThe largest baptistery in Italy, round and domed, where a guard sings a few notes every half hour to let you hear its echo. CamposantoThe marble cloister of a cemetery on the north side of the square, said to stand on earth brought back from Golgotha. Santa Maria della SpinaA tiny Gothic church on the bank of the Arno, all pinnacles and statues, once home to a thorn from Christ’s crown. Piazza dei CavalieriThe square of the Knights of Saint Stephen, with Vasari’s painted palace, now the Scuola Normale, and the tower where Count Ugolino starved. Borgo StrettoPisa’s old shopping street under the arcades, from the Ponte di Mezzo north into the medieval town. The LungarniThe streets along both banks of the Arno, lined with palazzi in ochre and pink, lit with thousands of candles every 16 June. Ponte di MezzoThe middle bridge, where the city has crossed the Arno since Roman times, and where the north and the south of Pisa fight every June. Logge di BanchiThe open loggia at the south end of the Ponte di Mezzo, built around 1605 for the silk and wool traders. TuttomondoKeith Haring’s last big mural, painted in 1989 on the back wall of the convent of Sant’Antonio, near the station. CecinaA thin, golden flatbread of chickpea flour, water and olive oil, baked in a copper pan in a wood oven and eaten hot with pepper.
