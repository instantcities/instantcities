# Site visuals

The four new sites were committed and pushed separately before this visual pass. All seven sites
now use their own daytime light and horizon, shared weathered materials and refreshed game images.

| Site | This pass | A useful next modelling pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taj Mahal | Warmer marble light, fine marble grain and veins, more people along the garden walks; the race retains the site's full theme. | Floral inlay panels and finer jali screens; garden planting drawn from reference photographs. |
| Angkor Wat | Weathered stone finish, light from the approach, clearer long views and visitors along the causeway; the race retains the site's full theme. | More rounded lotus towers, carved pediments and individual balusters in the galleries. |
| Pompeii | Warmer daylight, weathered stone on public monuments and a narrower Vesuvius silhouette; the existing street crowds remain. | More excavated wall and fresco detail at walking height; improved archaeological footprints for individual houses. |
| Stonehenge | An inner viewing walk with visitors, an open grass setting without generic trees inside the earthwork, weathered stone and softer daylight. | Refine individual sarsens from a measured stone plan; add small lichen patches and grass variation. |
| Giza | Warm desert light, mineral grain on the pyramids and Sphinx, more visitors along the circuit. | Model the mastabas as excavated tombs rather than generic buildings; refine the Sphinx's face and headdress. |
| Acropolis | A raised summit, bare stone ground, a low perimeter wall, fallen column drums and blocks, visitors and warmer marble light. | Replace the approximate hill with measured terrain; refine the Caryatids and the surviving pediment fragments. |
| Machu Picchu | A raised ridge, terraces, roofless rooms with weathered stone, visitors, clearer mountain profiles and an open central lawn. | Replace the approximate ridge with measured terrain and a more faithful plan of the ceremonial buildings. |

The relief is deliberately a small artistic height field, not a geographic elevation product.
The existing terrain renderer keeps riding and collision coordinates level, and draws the ground
relative to the rider. No claim of surveyed heights is made. The next substantial improvement
would be a shared terrain and monument reference workflow using properly licensed surveys and
photographs, beginning with Giza's tomb field and Machu Picchu's ridge.
