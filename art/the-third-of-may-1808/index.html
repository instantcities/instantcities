The Third of May 1808

El tres de mayo de 1808 en Madrid

Francisco de Goya, 1814

French soldiers shoot the people of Madrid in the night, a man in a white shirt flinging up his arms before them.

See it in 3D The Prado Madrid

On 2 May 1808 Madrid rose against Napoleon's army; that night and the next morning the French shot the prisoners they had taken. Six years later, when the French had gone, Goya painted the uprising and the executions.

The soldiers are a faceless row of backs and rifles; the man they aim at, lit by a lantern on the ground, holds out his arms like Christ on the cross, a wound in his palm. Its truth about war has been followed ever since, by Manet and by Picasso's Guernica. Stand before it in 3D ›
