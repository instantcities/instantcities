Comic-strip walls

Tintin, the Smurfs and Lucky Luke painted on the gable walls of the old town.

Brussels is the capital of the bande dessinée: Hergé, the creator of Tintin, was born here, and many great comics came out of the city. Since 1991 more than sixty gable walls have been painted with comic scenes.

A walk through the old town takes you past Tintin and Captain Haddock on a fire escape, Asterix, the Smurfs and Lucky Luke. The Comics Museum is in an Art Nouveau department store by Victor Horta. Ride through Brussels in 3D ›

More in Brussels

King’s HouseThe neo-Gothic house facing the town hall, home of the city museum and Manneken Pis’ wardrobe. Manneken PisA bronze boy barely half a metre tall, with a wardrobe of more than a thousand costumes. Galeries Royales Saint-HubertOne of the first covered shopping arcades in Europe, opened in 1847 under a glass roof. BourseThe old stock exchange of 1873, on a boulevard that has been free of cars since 2015. Cathedral of St Michael and St GudulaBrussels’ Gothic cathedral on the slope towards the upper town, where royal weddings are held. Mont des ArtsThe garden steps between the lower and the upper town, with a view over the city. SablonThe square of antique dealers and chocolatiers, below the Gothic church of Notre-Dame du Sablon. FriesThick fries fried twice in beef fat, served in a paper cone with mayonnaise. ChocolateThe praline was invented in Brussels, in the Galeries Saint-Hubert, in 1912.
